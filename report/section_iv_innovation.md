# IV. Innovation and Contributions

The task asks for a vehicle that follows a lane, detects obstacles and stops or avoids them. Standard building blocks exist for each part. Our contribution is in how they are combined, how the decisions are made safe, and how the system was tested. Six points stand out.

**A. A speed-aware stop-or-avoid decision that can change its mind.** The braking point is the stopping distance d = v·t_react + v²/(2a) + margin, so faster cars react earlier, and an overtake starts early enough to finish the lane change (v × 2.5 s). The state machine is not one-way: it holds a stop with hysteresis, aborts an overtake back behind the obstacle if the target lane closes, never overtakes something that may be moving, and switches to adaptive cruise (FOLLOW) behind a moving car. Every decision can be traced to one rule in the code.

**B. A map-guided LiDAR corridor instead of a box in front of the car.** The detector only looks inside a corridor that follows the shape of the lane (or the planned route) taken from the map, estimates the road height from the points next to that corridor, and accepts a detection only as a cluster of at least three points. Each of these choices came from a failure seen in CARLA (guardrails on curves, the terrain under an elevated highway, buildings beyond a turn). Together they removed almost all false alarms while the distance error stayed around 0.3–0.5 m.

**C. Overtaking that accounts for oncoming traffic.** The adjacent lane counts as free only if the nearest car in it is far enough away for the whole manoeuvre: nearest distance − closing speed × 9 s > 10 m. The closing speed comes from tracking the LiDAR distance over time. The answer must stay "free" for one second before the car pulls out. From this rule we derived a sensor requirement: about 150 m of visible free lane, which a 32-channel LiDAR does not provide reliably at urban scale but a 64-channel long-range unit does.

**D. Evaluation that measures perception, not only outcomes.** Every run logs the ground-truth values next to what the sensors reported, tick by tick, so perception errors (distance error, missed and false detections, lane-check disagreements) are measured and not guessed. Runs are seeded and reproducible across six towns and up to ten scenarios. A semantic LiDAR mode names the object behind every false alarm, which is how most of the bugs in point B were found. The camera lane detector was tuned on 1,674 automatically labelled frames, with a held-out test drive per town.

**E. Navigation through junctions with the same safety layer.** A route planner on the map's road graph and a pure-pursuit route follower drive the car through junctions and turns, slowing to 5 m/s near junctions and 4 m/s in sharp turns. The obstacle corridor follows the route, so the same detection and stop logic protects the car along the whole route, and overtaking is disabled near junctions.

**F. Engineering for a simulator that crashes.** Sensor data are read for the exact simulation frame (stale LiDAR sweeps had delayed braking by several frames), sensors are torn down in a safe order, results are written atomically, and a watchdog restarts CARLA when it dies, so a 200-run evaluation can finish unattended.
